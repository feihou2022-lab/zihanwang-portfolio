Research overview

Grounded in fieldwork in Xuhui District, the paper describes a mini-program intended to connect older adults and family caregivers with screening, information, care services, and mutual support.

Full title: Analysis of Pathways for Optimizing Care Services for Older Adults with Cognitive Impairment — A Case Study of Practical Mini-Program Development

Data & approach

The paper reports field investigations in four communities, five care institutions, and three community service centers; interviews with 11 professional social workers; and 196 valid responses from 200 distributed questionnaires. A team-led Version 1.0 pilot involved 68 older adults from three Xuhui communities.

What the source reports

  • The six modules cover simple screening, brain-health education, intervention courses, family care-bed applications, professional care institutions, and caregiver mutual-aid communities.
  • Pilot participants provided feedback on interface readability, navigation, functions, and privacy. The paper describes revisions informed by this feedback.
  • The project treats access to care as a coordination problem involving families, communities, and professional services.

Why it matters

The service-design question is how to make the next step in seeking help more understandable and accessible. The paper documents a proposed pathway and early user feedback.

Reading the evidence. The pilot is a usability and feedback exercise, not a controlled evaluation of clinical benefit or caregiver outcomes. This portfolio presents the paper; a live mini-program is not linked.
