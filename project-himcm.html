Research overview

The team paper models responder search routes in buildings during emergency scenarios. It combines graph-based routing with workload allocation and tests how results change under different assumptions.

Full title: An Evacuation Sweeping Strategy Based on Minimum Spanning Tree

Data & approach

The approach begins with depth-first search (DFS) in a simple single-floor layout. For more complex buildings, it uses Kruskal’s algorithm to construct a minimum spanning tree (MST), partitions the tree among responders, and examines visibility and search-efficiency parameters.

What the source reports

  • The model progresses from a simple office layout to modeled White House and five-story school-building cases.
  • Partitioning the search graph distributes work among responders; added factors include fire spread, smoke, and priority areas.
  • Sensitivity analysis explores how changes in visibility and search efficiency alter modeled sweep times.

Why it matters

This project extends the portfolio’s quantitative toolkit: translating a practical problem into explicit assumptions, a model, and tests of robustness.

Reading the evidence. Simulation results are not field-validated evacuation instructions or a guarantee of safety. The paper is a team submission and does not identify individual contributions.
