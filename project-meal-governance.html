Research overview

This coauthored paper examines the mismatch between administrative service boundaries and the places older residents actually live and access meals. The source retains anonymized place names: District P and City S.

Full title: Research on the Bidirectional Embedding Mechanism of Grassroots Administrative District Governance and Community Life Circle Services — A Case Study of Elderly Meal Assistance Services in District P of City S

Data & approach

The case analysis draws on collaborative governance, cross-boundary governance, and community life-circle perspectives. It develops a framework for connecting administrative governance with residents’ everyday service needs.

What the source reports

  • Differences in household registration, actual residence, and service location create challenges for access to nearby meal assistance.
  • The paper identifies uneven service resources, differing subsidy policies, information barriers, and cross-boundary settlement difficulties.
  • Proposed responses include coordinated policy design, multi-stakeholder governance, information sharing, and stronger channels for resident feedback.

Why it matters

Proximity alone does not ensure access. Institutional rules and coordination help determine whether a nearby service can actually be used.

Reading the evidence. This is a collaborative case study, not an experimental test of a policy intervention. The PDF lists Xiong Jing, Ding Yingjie, Wang Zihan, and Zhou Weijun as authors; it does not specify individual task contributions.
