Research overview

Zihan’s activity list describes her role as a research assistant analyzing the 769 survey responses on an SJTU team led by Professor Fan Yang. This study examines what active older adults in Shanghai need from public space, community services, and social support. It brings everyday accessibility into the discussion of cognitive-health-friendly environments.

Full title: Research on the Demand for Building Cognitive Health-Friendly Environments — A Case Study of Shanghai

Data & approach

The questionnaire was distributed through Wen Juan Xing with support from community social workers and volunteers. Respondents were aged 60 or above and able to move about and comprehend the questionnaire. The study collected 769 valid responses; the mean age was 70.5. Its recommendations also draw on interviews with 14 professional social workers.

What the source reports

  • Safer, smoother walking paths were selected by 47.20% of respondents as one of their top two improvement priorities.
  • More resting seats (29.78%) and clearer pictorial wayfinding (28.22%) followed. These percentages are multiple-choice responses and do not add up to 100%.
  • The report recommends incremental physical improvements, neighborhood support networks, and a coordinated service system that includes caregiver training and respite care.

Why it matters

Community design begins with ordinary routes: leaving the apartment, finding a place to rest, and navigating familiar streets. The research connects these everyday needs with family and community support.

Reading the evidence. The sampling criteria favored active older adults with normal mobility and comprehension, underrepresenting people with greater functional limitations. The results should not be treated as a representative estimate for all older adults or all people with dementia.
