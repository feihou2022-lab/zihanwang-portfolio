Research overview

As a research assistant on the SJTU team, Zihan analyzed source data and helped build the five-stage demand model, according to her activity list. The resulting report from the School of International and Public Affairs at Shanghai Jiao Tong University estimates eldercare workforce requirements in Xuhui District and examines workforce structure and working conditions.

Full title: Research on the Estimation of Elderly Care Needs and Strategy for Caregiver Team Construction in Xuhui District

Data & approach

The report combines statistical yearbooks, Shanghai eldercare service statistics, and survey data from 13 sub-districts and towns. It links five stages: older population, disability status, care hours, caregiver demand, and the supply-demand gap. Estimates use optimistic, baseline, and prudent scenarios.

What the source reports

  • Under the report’s stated assumptions, the 2030 caregiver gap is estimated at 114 in institutions and 414 in community and home-based settings. These are projections, not observed shortages in 2030.
  • The report states that women account for 94.48% of the surveyed in-service caregiver workforce, highlighting the gendered composition of paid care.
  • Recommendations address recruitment, professional development, coordination across care settings, and support for frontline workers.

Why it matters

Workforce planning makes care visible as time, skill, and labor. It also raises a question that connects to the documentary: whose work is recognized, and how is it supported?

Reading the evidence. The report is institutionally attributed. The supplied activity list describes Zihan’s role as research assistant: analyzing source data and helping build the five-stage model. This is a team report, not a sole-authored paper. Scenario estimates depend on population, service-share, and productivity assumptions. Paid-care workforce figures are not estimates of unpaid family caregiving.
